## One useful organic post

Nothing new to announce this week? You can still publish something useful.

Start with a question your audience asks. Write the direct answer, show one
example, and give the reader one step to try.

For example, "What can I post between product updates?" could become:

- A short demonstration of choosing a topic from a question bank.
- A text-led answer someone can understand without watching a talking head.
- A slide sequence explaining how to turn the question into a complete post.

Each version should teach something. Choose the format that makes the answer
easiest to use; you do not need to make all three.

Try this today: find one real question, write what the reader should learn,
and draft its answer before choosing a design or editor.

The guide includes a filled example, a blank planning brief, and a weekly
review process: [SaaS content marketing weekly plan](https://getugcpilot.com/guides/saas-content-marketing).

## Next topic 1: finding useful organic content ideas

Reader problem: a founder can produce content but does not know which
questions are worth answering.

Working angle: **How to Find Organic Content Ideas in Customer Questions**.
No volume, difficulty, or exact-title demand is established for this angle.

Outcome: a de-identified question bank and one answerable topic with evidence.

Questions to cover:

1. What counts as a useful audience question?
2. Where can a small team find questions with little existing traffic?
3. How should similar questions be grouped without losing the audience's words?
4. Which question deserves a full guide, a social post, an FAQ, or a support answer?
5. How do evidence readiness and intended audience change the priority?

Original value: show a small labeled example bank, explain two rejected topics,
and fill a selection worksheet. Use actual de-identified questions when available.

that section unless a distinct reader task justifies a separate URL.

## Next topic 2: making on-screen text readable on mobile

Reader problem: the message is clear in a script, but the finished video is
hard to read on a phone.

Working angle: **How to Make On-Screen Text Readable on Mobile**.
No volume, difficulty, or exact-title demand is established for this angle.

Outcome: a reviewed video and a repeatable check for text, background, and pacing.

Questions to cover:

1. Is there too much text to read comfortably before the scene changes?
2. Does the background reduce contrast or compete with the message?
3. Does the text obscure the important visual or conflict with platform controls?
4. How can a real phone preview reveal a problem the desktop editor hides?
5. What is a manual text overlay, and how does it differ from speech captions?

Original value: use an owned clip, show the same message before and after a
specific readability correction, and explain the decision. Do not invent a
universal minimum font size or a measured engagement lift. Use official
platform specifications and relevant accessibility guidance for concrete rules.

Product role: a verified Wall of text editing example can illustrate the
method after production acceptance. The article should also teach the checks
for readers using another editor. Screenshots remain a user-supplied dependency.
