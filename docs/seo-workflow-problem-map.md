# SEO reader problems mapped to the new workflows

Prepared 9 October 2026. This is an editorial opportunity map, not evidence of
customer outcomes or current Google rankings. Sources: current application
code, workflow release notes, relevant how-to pages, and a small US keyword
sample. Drafting and publication remain separate steps.

## Editorial decision

Start with a problem the reader already has and teach a useful method for
organic content. Explain what is happening, why it happens, and how to make a
better decision or produce a better result. Show a relevant UGC Pilot workflow
when it helps demonstrate the solution. Each article should leave the reader
with useful understanding or a usable output. A workflow name alone is not a
sufficient article topic.

This workflow map covers one part of the content program. Also publish
educational articles that answer relevant questions without requiring a
product walkthrough. The first three workflow-specific opportunities are Hook
video, Wall of text, and Slideshows. The existing SaaS content marketing guide
becomes the weekly planning hub. Target founders and small teams marketing a
SaaS product, website, or app.

## First article: Hook video and product demos

Audience: a founder with an existing app or SaaS screen recording.

Organic purpose: teach a useful product task through a clear short demonstration.
Begin with why an opening feels unclear, how to state the viewer's takeaway,
and how to match that promise to visible evidence. Give an editor-independent
outline before the optional UGC Pilot walkthrough. Do not assume paid ads or
equate the video's success with a signup.

Search-intent hypothesis: the reader wants a usable process for creating a
short product demonstration, including an opening that makes its value clear.
Some searches for product demos also concern longer sales videos; target-market
results must confirm the short-social angle before finalizing the title.

Suggested worked example:

1. Choose one customer task that the existing demo actually demonstrates.
2. Write an honest opening stating that problem and the promised demonstration.
3. Choose a hook reference and generate a suitable opening, or use owned footage.
4. Review the opening and add readable manual text where it helps.
5. Save the hook edits, then add the owned product demo in the optional Demo step.
6. Preview the hook-to-demo transition and save the final video.
7. Choose an appropriate connected account and schedule the saved output once
   that production path has been verified.

Evidence needed: the original demo, the actual opening, the Demo step showing
both segments, and the saved output. Show the same product task throughout.
An example opening could be "Still copying every request into your task list?"
only when the actual product demo proves how that work is reduced. Do not
invent retention lifts, automatic highlight selection, or time savings.

## Second article: Wall of text and faceless explanations

Audience: a founder who has something useful to explain but does not want to
record a talking-head video.

Suggested worked example: turn one genuine customer question into a short
answer using generated background footage or an owned clip, editable manual
text, a readable pace, and an appropriate next action. The article teaches
how to choose the message, shorten it, maintain contrast, and inspect mobile
readability. The background should support the explanation.

Teach the writing and readability decisions before the editor steps. The
finished example should give a complete answer to the customer question; a
promotional message alone does not fulfill this educational brief.

Evidence needed: the message brief, the background source, the actual text
editing controls, and the finished video. Explain that the new format uses
manual text overlays; it does not offer automatic speech transcription in
its editor. Lettering already embedded in source footage cannot be changed
as a separate text layer.

Keep "add text to video" and "text overlay video" as supporting concepts in
this article. A generic faceless-channel income guide would have a weaker
connection to the founder's product-marketing task.

## Third article: Slideshows and step-by-step explanations

Audience: a founder whose product idea is clearer when the reader can examine
one step at a time.

Teach how to sequence an explanation and judge whether each slide helps the
reader understand or act. A step-by-step tutorial or useful comparison is a
valid example; the slide sequence does not have to promote a product feature.

Suggested worked example: plan a five-slide story covering problem, context,
method, example, and next action. Choose a relevant slideshow reference,
recreate the slides in order, review each generated image, regenerate a weak
slide if needed, and save the sequence to Library. Use a real product capture
as evidence in the article; do not promise an automatic screenshot-to-slideshow
importer without verifying such a feature.

Evidence needed: the selected reference, the generated sequence, an individual
slide correction, and the saved Library sequence. Check generated lettering,
product details, and reading order. Text within generated slide images changes
through regeneration. This workflow saves an image sequence, not a slideshow
video. Scheduling examples require production checks for the chosen platform.

These are provider search-volume estimates, not measured site traffic, signup
forecasts, or guaranteed monthly totals. SE Ranking's response includes an
October history bucket although October is incomplete: September values were
170 for product demo video, 12,100 for faceless reels, and 180 for instagram
slideshow. Compare consistent completed periods before making volume-based
decisions. Provider agreement is not equivalent to independent customer
validation.

Data-quality notes:

- Serpstat reports difficulty 3 for faceless reels while SE Ranking reports 75.
  Difficulty scales and methods differ; no easy-ranking claim is justified.
- Serpstat's faceless row also has an unexpected relationship between its
  regional count and wider count. Preserve both raw fields rather than silently
  treating them as interchangeable.
- Serpstat labels the English phrase "add text to video" as Russian and reports
  1,600; SE Ranking reports 220 currently and 1,300 for September. Exclude the
  Serpstat row from English prioritization until clarified.
- Four Serpstat inputs returned no row: instagram reel hooks, video hook
  generator, faceless instagram reels, and ai influencer generator. Missing
  provider data does not establish zero demand.
- The very small "repurpose video" estimate does not establish that founders
  have no need to reuse footage. Intent and customer evidence still matter.
- Advertising competition is not organic ranking difficulty. The three
  proposed article titles still need specific query and target-market checks.

Reviewed how-to sources support an inferred practical intent:

- [FrameCompose: writing hooks for short videos](https://www.framecompose.com/guides/how-to-write-hooks-for-short-videos) discusses connecting a concrete opening to demonstrable evidence, including product demos.
- [Async: faceless Reels without filming yourself](https://async.com/blog/faceless-reels-without-filming-yourself/) describes using existing visuals and on-screen text for product-led explanations.
- [Google's helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) supports writing for an intended audience and showing useful first-hand experience.

These source observations inform article intent, not a verified Google
ranking list or evidence that UGC Pilot users experience these problems.
Validate the language against customer questions and Search Console queries
as those become available.

## Repeatable article acceptance and evaluation

Use the agreed five steps: select the problem, research intent, build a brief,
draft/review with Agentic SEO Skill, then publish/evaluate. The skill helps
structure and review the article; product truth and search evidence require
independent checks.

For each workflow article, include:

1. An explanation of the problem and a practical method the reader can apply
   with their existing tools, followed by a concrete workflow example.
2. A worked example with real screenshots or video and honest limitations.
3. A copyable brief, script, or slide outline that helps the reader act.
4. Contextual links to the weekly hub and the verified product destination.
5. A recorded baseline for relevant search impressions/clicks and completed
   organic signups, with unavailable data labeled.

Measure article-to-workflow visits, successful saved output in the relevant
workflow, and completed signup where instrumentation permits. Scheduling can
be a later activation signal. These are proposed measures, not claims that
events already exist. Judge useful discovery and product evaluation alongside
rankings; an article's connection to the product does not guarantee rankings.

For educational articles, also check whether the answer is understandable,
complete, and reproducible without signing up. Reader feedback and follow-up
questions can reveal gaps. Where available, evaluate organic search relevance,
engagement, and useful next actions according to the article's purpose; signup
is not the sole definition of success for an educational article.
