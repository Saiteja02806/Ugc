# Draft: Keep track of source inspiration, permissions and your final creative

Unpublished: this draft uses a fictional planning example and needs the original, permissioned evidence specified in the content plan before release.

## Record the source before you remix

A reference can inform pacing, story structure or presentation, but it may also contain another person’s footage, words, music or likeness. Record where you found it and who owns the components before making a reuse decision. A public link or the ability to view an asset does not by itself establish permission to copy it.

This guide offers an operational checklist, not a legal determination. Licensing terms and permissions vary. When rights are unclear, use original or appropriately licensed material or seek advice.

## Create a practical provenance record

For each reference, record its source URL, creator or account if known, date accessed, elements you want to use, the permission or license and its limits, and where that evidence is stored. Separate the license for video from any music, spoken words or on-screen text if they have different owners. Do not record private customer details in a public brief.

For an illustrative fictional example, a founder notes that a public video uses a surprised opening and a product reveal. The founder records the link for research, writes a new script based on their own verified feature, and uses their own footage. No claim is made that the original video is licensed for reuse.

## Decide what to create independently

Describe an idea in functional terms such as “reaction followed by screen proof.” Write original words rather than copying a creator’s full overlay. Use an owned presenter or a properly licensed asset, and create your own product recording. If a particular video, quote, sound or likeness is essential, check the specific authorization and scope before using it.

If permission is denied, unavailable or limited to a different channel, remove that element or request suitable permission. Do not assume credit alone replaces permission.

## Recheck the final asset

Match the finished export against the record: every third-party element should have a source and permission that covers the intended use. Verify product claims independently; a reference creator’s claims are not evidence about your application. Store the final asset and version alongside the provenance record so a later editor can repeat the review.

An internal checklist can reduce mistakes, but it cannot guarantee rights clearance. Seek qualified advice when a proposed use or license is uncertain.
