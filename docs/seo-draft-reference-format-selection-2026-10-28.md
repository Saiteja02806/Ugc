# Draft: Choose hook, talking-head, wall text or slideshow from a reference idea

**Unpublished planning draft.** This article uses clearly fictional examples. Before publishing, complete the original-evidence gate: Same business brief expressed in distinct full formats with choice rationale.

## Extract the task before selecting a reference format

A reference can suggest a format, but the format should serve your viewer’s task and your actual product. Identify the verified feature, the proof available and whether the reader needs to see a screen action, read a short story or follow several steps. Record the reference source and use only assets you have permission to reuse.

The examples below are fictional plans for NorthStar Notes, a notes app with a call note and next-step section. They are not finished assets or measured results.

## Express the same brief three ways

Reaction plus demo: “wait, the next steps are right here,” followed by a real recording that opens the note and holds the section. Choose this when the feature can be understood visually in a short sequence.

Wall of text: “the call is over. the follow-up is tomorrow. open the project note and check the next-step section.” Place the words over calm permissioned footage. Choose this when the situation and written observation are the main message.

Slideshow: “where did the next step go?” then the post-call situation, open the project note, check the section, and start with the next action. Choose this when the reader benefits from separate beats or instructions.

## Match proof and production constraints

A generated background illustrates a scene; it does not demonstrate your software. Use an actual sanitized screen recording or screenshot when the message depends on showing the interface. Confirm labels, access conditions and whether the user must create the note manually. If a reference depends on a feature you do not have, change the claim rather than copying it.

Use original text and footage or obtain the appropriate permission for each reused element. A popular reference does not establish permission or guarantee that your version will have similar results.

## Make a decision and review it

Choose the format that most directly explains the task with the proof you can supply. If all three contain the same words and no distinct purpose, develop one useful asset rather than making near-duplicates. Preview the final version on a phone and ask an unfamiliar viewer to identify the product action.

Record the chosen concept and the actual review feedback. Treat audience fit and performance as hypotheses until you observe real data.

## Example checklist

Format | What carries the message | Proof or review
--- | --- | ---
Reaction plus demo | Opening and visible app action | Can the feature be understood on screen?
Wall of text | Written situation and supported fact | Can the overlay be read in time?
Slideshow | A sequence of separate beats | Does each slide advance the task?
No suitable format | The product cannot support the claim | Revise the message or reject the reference
